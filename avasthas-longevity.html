This page covers planetary avasthas (states) — which modify how fully a planet expresses — and the longevity chapters, which are presented as classical doctrine only, with deliberate care.

Avasthas — the states of a planet

A planet with good dignity but a poor 'state' may under-deliver, and vice versa. BPHS gives five parallel avastha schemes; read them as modifiers layered on dignity and strength.

Baladi (5 — the 'ages') — Bala (infant), Kumara (child), Yuva (adult, fullest power), Vriddha (old), Mrita (dead) — assigned by the planet's degrees within its sign; a 'youth' planet gives the most, a 'dead' planet the least.

Jagradadi (3) — Jagrat (awake — in own/exaltation), Swapna (dreaming — in a friend's/neutral sign), Sushupti (sleeping — in an enemy's/debilitation sign): the alertness of the effect.

Deeptadi (9) — nine states blending dignity and association — e.g. Deepta (exalted, radiant), Swastha (own sign, at ease), Mudita (in a friend's sign, glad) … through Khala, Kopa and Bheeta (afflicted, agitated, fearful).

Lajjitadi (6) — situational states — Lajjita (ashamed), Garvita (proud), Kshudita (hungry), Trishita (thirsty), Mudita (delighted), Kshobhita (agitated) — from placement, company and aspect.

Sayanadi (12) — twelve postural states (reclining, sitting, standing, walking …) computed from a formula involving the planet, sign and the person's name-syllable, each with detailed results, and each further graded by a Cheshta / Drishti / Vicheshta sub-state that makes the result full, medium or nil.

Source BPHS, Vol. 1, Ch. 45 (Avasthas — Baladi, Jagradadi, Deeptadi, Lajjitadi, and the twelve Sayanadi states with their sub-state modifiers and per-planet effects).

Study note. For everyday reading the first three schemes carry the load: is the planet 'young' (Baladi), 'awake' (Jagradadi), and 'radiant/at-ease' vs 'agitated' (Deeptadi)? The Sayanadi scheme is powerful but calculation-heavy and name-dependent.

Longevity (Ayurdaya)

Handle with care. BPHS presents longevity as traditional astrological doctrine. Do not compute or predict a lifespan or a time of death for anyone — the methods below are described so the classical reasoning is intelligible, not to apply to a real person. Any genuine concern about health or mortality belongs with qualified medical professionals.

The text gives three principal methods and a threefold outcome band:

MethodBasis
Pindayuyear-contributions from the seven planets by their exaltation/debilitation position, plus the Lagna, with reductions
Nisargayuthe planets' fixed natural (naisargika) year-allotments
Amsayuderived from the total longitudes / navamsa positions of the planets and Lagna
  • At deep exaltation the seven planets (Sun→Saturn) contribute 19, 25, 15, 12, 15, 21, 20 years; halved at deep debilitation, and pro-rated in between.
  • Reductions apply — for planets in the 'visible' (upper) half of the chart, for malefics in the ascendant, and by other rules — before a figure is reached.
  • The result is classed Alpayu (short), Madhyayu (middle) or Purnayu (full); even choosing which method applies is a careful, disputed judgement in the text, with special rules for Saturn and Jupiter and for 'doubtful' cases.

Source BPHS, Vol. 1, Ch. 43 (Longevity — Pindayu, Nisargayu, Amsayu; the contribution figures 19/25/15/12/15/21/20; the reductions; the three bands; and the choice-of-method rules).

Balarishta (infant vulnerability)

Handle with care. The chapters also treat Balarishta — combinations classically read for danger in infancy. These carry many cancellations (benefic aspects, a strong Lagna and Moon) and must be treated purely as historical doctrine, never as a prediction about a child.

Maraka principles

Maraka ('that which can end') is the technical class of houses/lords BPHS links with the close of a life-span in longevity analysis — chiefly:

  • the 2nd and 7th houses, their lords, and planets associated with or aspecting them;
  • the Badhaka lord (the 'obstructing' house — 11th for movable, 9th for fixed, 7th for dual Lagnas);
  • malefic dasha/antardasha periods of these lords, in a chart already indicating a completed span.
Handle with care. Maraka is a doctrinal classification for understanding the tradition's internal logic — not a tool for forecasting death or harm for any individual. Keep it academic; do not apply it personally, and direct real concerns to medical professionals.

Source BPHS, Vol. 1, Ch. 44 (Maraka planets — marakas by lordship, the badhaka, maraka-dasha timing, and related material, presented here as theory only).

Study note. The honest use of these chapters is to grasp how the classical system reasons about vitality and resilience — which strengths protect, which afflictions strain — and to feed that into constructive, non-fatalistic reflection, never into a countdown.
