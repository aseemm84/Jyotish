Dignity measures the quality of the ground a planet stands on. Strength (Shadbala) says how loudly it can speak; dignity says whether what it says is sweet or sour. The two are read together.

Exaltation, debilitation, Moolatrikona & own sign

Each of the seven planets has an exaltation sign (with a precise deep degree of maximum power), a debilitation sign exactly opposite at the same degree, a Moolatrikona (root-trine — nearly as strong as exaltation), and own sign(s).

The strength ladder inside a sign

From strongest to weakest positional footing: exalted → Moolatrikona → own → great friend → friend → neutral → enemy → great enemy → debilitated. This exact ladder is scored in Saptavargaja Bala (see Strength).

Rahu and Ketu — the nodes

BPHS states plainly that there are different views on the exaltation and debilitation of the nodes, and it does not fix a single standard. What the text does note is a co-lordship: Rahu co-signifies Aquarius and Ketu co-signifies Scorpio. The most widely used tradition adds the exaltations below — use them knowingly, as tradition rather than settled BPHS doctrine.

NodeExaltation (tradition)Debilitation (tradition)BPHS co-lordship
RahuTaurus (some say Gemini)Scorpio (some say Sagittarius)co-signifies / ‘rules’ Aquarius (with Saturn)
KetuScorpio (some say Sagittarius)Taurus (some say Gemini)co-signifies / ‘rules’ Scorpio (with Mars)
  • Because the nodes have no undisputed own sign, many astrologers judge them chiefly by their dispositor (the lord of the sign they occupy) and by conjunctions and aspects.
  • Rahu is often read as behaving like Saturn, and Ketu like Mars, when a functional flavour is needed.
  • The nodes are not scored in Shadbala and are not treated as combust — they are points, not bodies.

Source BPHS, Vol. 1, Ch. 3 (‘as for the exaltations and debilitation of the nodes, there are different views’); co-lordship of Aquarius (Rahu) and Scorpio (Ketu) noted in Ch. 29 and Ch. 46.

Handle with care. Node dignity is genuinely unsettled across texts and lineages. State which convention you are using, and lean on dispositor and association rather than treating a node ‘exaltation’ as decisive.

Friendship and enmity

Natural (Naisargika) friendship

Parashara gives a positional rule: from a planet's Moolatrikona sign, the lords of the 2nd, 4th, 5th, 8th, 9th and 12th are friends (plus the lord of its exaltation sign); the lords of the 3rd, 6th, 7th, 10th and 11th are enemies; a lord arising both ways is neutral.

Source BPHS, Vol. 1, Ch. 3, v. 55 (the Moolatrikona-based natural friendship formula).

Temporal (Tatkalika) friendship

Relative to a planet's own position in the chart, planets in the 2nd, 3rd, 4th, 10th, 11th and 12th from it are temporary friends; those in the 1st, 5th, 6th, 7th, 8th and 9th are temporary enemies. This changes chart to chart.

Compound (Panchadha) relationship

Combine natural + temporal to get the fivefold relationship used in Saptavargaja Bala:

Natural + TemporalResult
friend + friendgreat friend (Adhimitra)
friend + enemy / enemy + friendneutral (Sama)
neutral + friendfriend
neutral + enemyenemy
enemy + enemygreat enemy (Adhishatru)

Combustion, planetary war & retrogression

Combustion (Astangata) — a planet too near the Sun is 'burnt' — its results are scorched. The nodes are exempt.

GrahaCombustion orbIf retrograde
Moon12°—
Mars17°—
Mercury14°12° (retrograde)
Jupiter11°—
Venus10°8° (retrograde)
Saturn16°—

Planetary war (Graha Yuddha) — when two non-node planets are very close (within ~1°), the one with the lower longitude (or brighter) 'wins'; the loser is weakened. Scored inside Cheshta Bala.

Retrogression (Vakra) — a retrograde planet is considered strong in motion (high Cheshta Bala) and its significations intensify, though its expression can be inward or repeating.

Source BPHS, Vol. 1 — combustion orbs table; planetary war and retrograde strength are quantified under Cheshta Bala, Ch. 27–28.

Cancellation and the reality check

Dignity is a starting condition, not a verdict. Neechabhanga (cancellation of debilitation) is common — e.g. when the debilitated planet's dispositor, or the planet that would be exalted in that sign, is in a kendra from the Lagna or the Moon; then a debilitated planet can behave powerfully. Equally, an exalted planet that is combust, in a dusthana, or hemmed by malefics may deliver little.

Handle with care. Never read exaltation as guaranteed good or debilitation as doom. Always combine dignity with house ownership, placement, aspects, strength and dasha before drawing a conclusion.
Study note. Practical order for judging a planet's dignity: (1) note its sign — exalted / own / MT / friend / neutral / enemy / debilitated; (2) check for neechabhanga or affliction that flips the first read; (3) confirm in the Navamsa (a planet dignified in both D1 and D9 is genuinely strong; vargottama especially).
