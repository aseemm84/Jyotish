The 108 Nakshatra Padas & the Nine Grahas

Every pada — its navamsa, lord, deity, symbol, gana and syllable — plus, for each, how all nine planets tend to behave when placed there.

● Structure — BPHS

27×4 padas, pada = navamsa (D9), spans, Vimshottari lords, deities. Also the exaltation & natural-friendship tables the planet grid uses. (Vol. 1 Ch. 3–6; Vol. 2 Ch. 46.)

● Symbolism — classical, not BPHS

Symbols, gana, nakshatra character, naming syllables — stable across Brihat Jataka & Phaladeepika, but outside the BPHS verses.

● Graha grid — derived, not quoted

No classical text fixes a verdict for all 972 planet×pada cells. Each is synthesised from: planet karaka × its D9 dignity here × nakshatra theme × dispositor. Reproducible, not a citation.

● Read as tendencies

These lean toward a direction; they are not fortunes. Judge always with the whole chart, strength & dasha. Nodes have no sign dignity — read via dispositor, with extra caution.

The graha grid — exactly how each cell is built

For a classical planet in a pada: its D9 dignity is read in the pada's navamsa sign using BPHS exaltation/debilitation, own-sign and natural-friendship rules; that sets the strength phrase. The planet's karaka themes are then filtered through the nakshatra's character, and the dispositor (the nakshatra's Vimshottari lord) is checked as friend/enemy/neutral to the planet. Rahu and Ketu have no own sign, so their cells carry no dignity verdict — they amplify (Rahu) or spiritualise/detach (Ketu) the nakshatra theme and are explicitly routed through the dispositor, as BPHS counsels extra caution with the nodes.

This composition is my educational synthesis. It is not a quotation from BPHS or any other text, and different authors would phrase the blend differently. Treat each cell as a first-order tendency to test against the full chart, not a verdict.

The bhava (house) layer

Pick a house and each planet cell gains a second line. House significations follow BPHS (bhava chapters, Vol. 1 Ch. 11 onward). The planet's tendency in that house is derived from its karaka nature, the house's significations, and the house's functional class — kendra, trikona, dusthana, maraka, upachaya — plus dig bala (directional strength: Sun/Mars in the 10th, Moon/Venus in the 4th, Mercury/Jupiter in the 1st, Saturn in the 7th), all BPHS concepts. The house depends on the lagna, so the same planet-in-pada sits in different bhavas for different ascendants — treat this as a general tendency to place against a real chart. Pada (intrinsic nature) and bhava (life-area) are independent axes; the full reading is their combination.
