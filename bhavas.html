A bhava (house) is an arena of life counted from the Lagna. Twelve houses cover the whole of experience. Each is judged by four things together: its natural significations, its lord's condition and placement, its occupants, and the aspects it receives — cross-checked with the relevant karaka and divisional chart.

Source BPHS, Vol. 1 — bhava significations (Ch. 11 onward) and house classifications (Ch. 7, vv. 33–36); bhava-lord placements (Ch. 24).

Translator / commentary. A house's meanings overlap and extend — e.g. the 4th covers mother, home, land, vehicles, schooling and 'the end of matters'; the 8th covers death, chronic illness, inheritance, and the occult. Read the cluster, then let the lord, occupants, aspects and dasha decide which strands activate.

Functional classes of the houses

Kendra (1,4,7,10) — angles — the load-bearing pillars; planets here are strong and visible. Vishnu's places.

Trikona (1,5,9) — trines — dharma and fortune; the most auspicious houses. Lakshmi's places.

Dusthana / Trika (6,8,12) — houses of difficulty — debt & disease, upheaval & death, loss & expense.

Upachaya (3,6,10,11) — 'growth' houses — their matters improve with time and effort; malefics do well here.

Maraka (2,7) — 'life-supporting / life-ending' houses used in longevity timing (see the Longevity page).

Panapara / Apoklima — succedent (2,5,8,11) and cadent (3,6,9,12) houses — a secondary strength gradation used in Kendradi Bala.

Source BPHS, Vol. 1, Ch. 7, vv. 33–36 (Kendra, Panapara/Apoklima, Kona, Trika, Chaturasra, Upachaya).

Bhava lords and their placements

A house-lord carries that house's matters wherever it travels, linking the two houses. BPHS gives a long chapter on the effect of each lord in each house, governed by two cautions:

  • Dual lordship. Except the Sun and Moon, every planet owns two houses, so its result blends both ownerships — weigh them together.
  • Strength scales the result. The lord delivers its indicated effect in full, half, or a quarter as it is strong, medium, or weak.
Worked example. Saturn in the 5th for a Pisces Lagna owns the 11th and 12th. As 11th-lord in the 5th it can give children and gains through them; as 12th-lord it can deny or delay progeny and turn the person toward shrines and remedies. BPHS resolves the contradiction by weighing strength and cross-checking Jupiter (progeny karaka) and the Moon (5th-lord) — then declares the blended result.

Source BPHS, Vol. 1, Ch. 24 (effects of each bhava-lord in each bhava; the dual-lordship resolution and the full/half/quarter strength-scaling rule).

Reading the strength of a house (Bhava Bala)

A house itself is scored, not only its lord — combining the strength of its lord, of the planets occupying and aspecting it, and of its natural karaka. A strong house delivers; a weak one under-delivers even with a good lord. (The full Bhava Bala method sits on the Strength page.)

When a house prospers or breaks

its lord is strong, dignified, well-placed and unafflicted; the house holds or is aspected by a benefic; the lord occupies a good state and a favourable house (e.g. well-placed from the 10th)its lord is weak, combust, debilitated, defeated in war or 'asleep'; the lord joins malefics or difficult-house lords; neither the house nor its lord receives any benefic influence

Source BPHS, Vol. 1 — bhava chapters (principles of prosperity and damage of a house through its lord's condition and benefic/malefic influence).

Study note. Resist ‘benefic good, malefic bad’. Ownership from this Lagna is decisive: a strong Saturn owning trines can be a blessing, while a weak Venus owning a maraka and a dusthana can trouble. Judge the function, not the label.
